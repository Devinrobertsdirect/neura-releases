# Companion robot buying checklist

**Disclosure:** Devin Roberts is developing Nobi and has a [commercial Etsy listing](https://nobibot.etsy.com/listing/4578776218). This guide is published by the project, not an independent product review. It does not recommend buying Nobi or rank the examples below.

Sources checked November 10, 2026. Product examples describe vendor documentation, not hands-on validation. A live page may contain older information; confirm the exact model, firmware and offer before buying.

## Start with the experience you want

Write down three things you want the robot to do, such as react to touch, hold a conversation or run code you write. Then distinguish:

- **Finished standalone desk companion:** supplied as an assembled product for its advertised everyday interactions. Ask which tasks work without another device or service; “assembled” does not establish that.
- **Cloud-connected AI product:** some desired functions depend on an online service. Identify those functions and the provider before judging the experience.
- **Programmable kit or development project:** ownership may involve assembly, calibration, coding or unfinished integration. Ask what work remains and who does it.

These categories can overlap. A pre-assembled robot can still be programmable and depend on cloud services.

## Check exactly what you receive

- [ ] Record the model, hardware revision and selected bundle, not just the product name.
- [ ] Get an itemized list: assembled robot or parts, battery, charging equipment, computer board, storage, accessories and software access.
- [ ] Separate included items from required extras and optional upgrades. Confirm assembly, calibration and setup responsibilities.

**Example:** Petoi's Bittle X page lists construction and pre-assembled V2 variants. Its getting-started documentation directs V2 owners to a separate guide. Check the manual for the actual revision you will receive. [Bittle X variants](https://www.petoi.com/products/petoi-robot-dog-bittle-x-voice-controlled), [Petoi getting started](https://docs.petoi.com/getting-started-guide).

## Ask what the demonstration proves

- [ ] Look for the actual physical configuration performing your three desired tasks, with the firmware version, connections and required accessories identified.
- [ ] Distinguish a render, browser animation, scripted interaction, prototype and current physical demonstration.
- [ ] Request evidence of repeatable operation, including recovery after a restart. Ask which failures or limits the demonstration leaves out.
- [ ] Keep promised updates separate from functions available now.

## Map dependencies by task

For each must-have function, write: **function → device or service needed → account or key → ongoing cost → behavior when unavailable**.

- [ ] Check setup, everyday use, updates and recovery separately. Which need a phone, supported computer, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi or internet?
- [ ] Ask whether an account, your own API key, paid subscription or usage credits are required. Who bills you, and can usage be limited?
- [ ] Ask what remains usable when the connection fails, a subscription ends or a service closes. Request feature-specific answers rather than a single “offline” label.

**Example:** EnergizeLab's Eilik FAQ says Eilik can operate without Wi-Fi or internet. Its separate update instructions require a USB-connected computer and a stable network during updating. Daily operation and maintenance have different requirements; these are vendor statements, not tests performed for this guide. [Eilik FAQ, dated July 2022](https://energizelab.com/supportview/eilik007), [update instructions, dated August 2023](https://energizelab.com/supportview/tutorial002).

## Check data handling and long-term ownership

- [ ] For microphones, cameras, names and conversation history, ask what is collected, where it is processed and stored, who receives it, how long it is kept and how deletion works. Read the policy for the device and any connected service.
- [ ] Ask how to disable recording or transmission and verify that control. An animation, local processor or offline feature alone does not establish the complete data flow.
- [ ] Find the support route, return terms, warranty exclusions and repair location before paying. Ask about replacement batteries, motors and other wear parts, repair instructions and costs.
- [ ] Ask whether ownership can transfer, accounts can be removed and software can be restored. Check the update history and documented support period without assuming future updates.

**Example:** LivingAI's support form requests an order number and an EMO ID, which it says appears on the app's connection page. Before buying used, check what records and app access you will receive, and confirm warranty transferability separately. [LivingAI support](https://living.ai/support/).

## Apply the same questions to Nobi

Nobi remains in development. The current full-size design is based on Raspberry Pi 4; final hardware configuration, fit and complete-build testing remain unverified. The [development reference](https://github.com/Devinrobertsdirect/neura-releases/blob/main/press/README.md) includes a July 2026 incomplete prototype photograph with no installed display. It is historical evidence, not current finished-product proof.

The [existing public face demo](https://developmentindustries.org/#top) lets you tap Nobi and choose eye colours. It demonstrates browser-rendered expressions, not finished hardware or a live AI conversation. Software-interface animation also does not establish physical operation. Check the commercial listing linked above and ask the seller for current included items, dependencies and physical test evidence before deciding.

**Decision rule:** Keep a short record of each must-have function, supporting evidence, unanswered questions and total ownership cost. If a must-have depends on an unanswered question or a future feature, resolve it before buying.
